# Portfolio Website Design Brief

## 1. Project overview

Create a personal portfolio website for a physics graduate with approximately three years of professional experience in medical physics who is transitioning towards environmental and sustainability-related work.

The website should position her as a science-trained professional who is interested in contributing to the planning, coordination, communication, and delivery of environmental projects. Her research and medical physics background should remain visible and valuable, but the website should not present her as an established environmental project manager or sustainability specialist unless her CV and work samples substantiate that experience.

**Primary purpose:** help international recruiters, environmental organisations, project teams, research groups, and potential collaborators understand her background, review examples of her work, and contact her about relevant opportunities.

**Target role families:**

- Environmental Project Officer
- Sustainability Project Officer
- Project or Programme Coordinator
- Environmental Programme Assistant or Project Assistant
- Junior Project Manager, where the role is appropriate to her actual experience
- Research or project support roles connected to environmental and sustainability work

These are target roles, not claims about her current job title or experience. Keep the positioning broad enough to support opportunities across different countries and environmental sectors.

**Primary conversion:** a relevant visitor contacts her or requests her CV. A CV download should only be enabled after she approves the exact file for public sharing.

## 2. Audience and language

Design primarily for:

- International recruiters and hiring managers in environmental organisations, sustainability teams, consultancies, NGOs, research groups, and public-interest projects.
- Project leads who need people able to organise information, communicate technical topics, support project delivery, and work with different stakeholders.
- Researchers and collaborators who want to assess her scientific foundation and the quality of her work.

The website's public-facing language should be **English**. Use globally understandable wording and avoid country-specific job terminology, spelling conventions, dates, or assumptions unless a particular opportunity requires them. Keep the language accessible to readers who are not physicists.

Most visitors will scan rather than read every page. Her professional direction, evidence of work, and contact route should be clear within the first few seconds.

## 3. Positioning and content principles

### Core positioning

Use the following as a working direction and refine it once the CV and portfolio materials have been reviewed:

> A physics graduate with three years of medical physics experience, bringing scientific thinking, research skills, and clear communication to environmental and sustainability projects.

Possible hero headline:

> **Turning scientific thinking into practical environmental action.**

Possible supporting copy:

> With a background in physics and three years of experience in medical physics, I am now focused on environmental and sustainability-related project work. My portfolio brings together research, technical communication, project contributions, and visual materials that show how I approach complex topics and communicate them clearly.

These are draft copy directions, not verified biography. Adjust them to the evidence in her CV and supplied work. Do not claim project-management responsibility, environmental expertise, stakeholder ownership, or measurable impact unless a real example supports it.

### The career narrative

The story should connect three parts:

1. **Scientific foundation:** physics education, research methods, analytical thinking, and medical physics experience.
2. **Transferable professional practice:** examples of communication, documentation, coordination, teamwork, planning, problem solving, presentation, quality processes, or technical work, but only where her experience demonstrates them.
3. **Environmental direction:** a clear and positive interest in contributing to environmental and sustainability projects, with specific themes added only after she confirms them.

Treat the transition as a deliberate next step. Do not frame her previous career as irrelevant, and do not force every medical physics task into an environmental narrative. Show the transferable value honestly.

### Potential environmental themes

Keep the site flexible across environmental work. Possible themes to explore with her include environmental education and awareness, waste and circular economy, conservation, ecosystem restoration, climate adaptation, environmental monitoring, community-based initiatives, resource efficiency, and sustainable healthcare. These are examples for future positioning, not assumed specialisms. Only feature themes she confirms or can evidence through actual work.

### Voice

- Confident, thoughtful, curious, practical, and approachable.
- Modern and human rather than corporate or overly academic.
- Explain technical ideas clearly without diluting the substance.
- Use first person where it makes the About page and project reflections more personal.
- Prefer concrete examples to generic phrases such as “passionate, dynamic, results-driven professional.”
- Avoid inflated language such as “expert,” “leader,” “strategist,” or “project manager” unless substantiated.

## 4. Visual direction

### Creative direction: earthy editorial meets scientific storytelling

Build a modern portfolio with a warm, grounded visual identity inspired by natural materials, landscape textures, field notebooks, printed research publications, and contemporary independent studios. The site should feel creative and welcoming without losing the clarity and credibility expected of a science-trained professional.

Use an **earth-tone palette** as the foundation. Warm parchment, oat, stone, olive, muted moss, terracotta, and ochre should create a sense of warmth and environmental connection. Keep the strongest colours for deliberate emphasis, navigation states, and calls to action. The design should not become a green-themed environmental template: earth tones should be visible across the entire system, with olive acting as one accent rather than the only identity.

The overall impression should be:

- **Grounded:** warm neutrals and natural, mineral-inspired colours rather than stark white and bright green.
- **Creative:** editorial composition, expressive but controlled typography, layered project previews, and varied image proportions.
- **Scientific:** precise alignment, clear captions, readable metadata, evidence-led writing, and structured case studies.
- **Personal:** a recognisable individual voice and a portfolio that feels curated rather than generated from a generic template.
- **Environmental:** a subtle connection to landscapes, materials, ecology, and fieldwork without relying on cliché leaf illustrations or stock environmental imagery.

Avoid making the site feel like a university lab profile, a conventional corporate ESG consultancy website, or a wellness brand. It should bridge creative communication and scientific credibility.

### Earth-tone colour palette

Use the following as the initial design tokens. The palette is intentionally warm and muted, with dark olive for key actions, terracotta for selective emphasis, and parchment-based neutrals for the main surfaces.

| Token | Hex | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| `parchment` | `#F2EDE3` | Main page background |
| `ivory` | `#FAF8F2` | Cards, document panels, and raised surfaces |
| `ink` | `#292A24` | Primary text and headings |
| `muted` | `#746F63` | Supporting text, captions, and metadata |
| `olive` | `#596247` | Primary accent, links, buttons, and active states |
| `moss` | `#7A8064` | Secondary accent and selected graphic details |
| `sage` | `#D7D9C9` | Soft tinted surfaces and supporting panels |
| `terracotta` | `#B66E52` | Small highlights, labels, and selected calls to action |
| `ochre` | `#C29A5B` | Minor highlights, tags, and editorial details |
| `clay` | `#A99B87` | Secondary neutral surfaces and dividers |
| `line` | `#DED7CA` | Borders, rules, and separators |

Use `parchment` as the dominant canvas and `ivory` for contrast between content areas. Use `olive` for the main button and high-priority links. `terracotta` and `ochre` are supporting accents, not competing primary colours. `sage` and `clay` can distinguish selected cards or sections without introducing loud blocks of colour.

Keep text contrast accessible. Do not use pale sage, ochre, or terracotta for small text on a light background unless the contrast is verified. Avoid pure black, pure white as the dominant palette, saturated lime green, neon accents, glossy gradients, and large areas of dark green. A very subtle paper-like background texture is acceptable only if it does not reduce readability or affect performance; a flat colour is preferable to a noisy texture.

### Colour application and component guidance

- Header and navigation: parchment or ivory background, ink text, olive active state, and a fine clay-toned divider.
- Primary button: olive background with ivory text. Hover and focus states should remain clearly distinguishable.
- Secondary button: transparent or ivory background with an olive border and ink text.
- Project cards: ivory surfaces with thin `line` borders. Use small terracotta, moss, or ochre category markers sparingly.
- Section backgrounds: alternate parchment and ivory; use sage for occasional feature panels or quote sections.
- Metadata and captions: muted ink, never low-contrast pale brown.
- Footer: ink or deep olive background may be used as a single grounding element, with accessible ivory text and restrained terracotta detail.

### Typography

Use a clear sans-serif for body copy and a more expressive editorial face for large headings if it strengthens the visual identity.

Suggested direction:

- Headings: `Manrope`, `DM Sans`, or a similar modern family with strong display weights.
- Body: `DM Sans`, `Inter`, or another highly readable sans-serif.
- Optional editorial accent: a restrained serif such as `Lora` or `DM Serif Display` for selected large headings, pull quotes, or article titles. Do not use it for dense body copy or every heading.
- Metadata: compact but readable, with adequate contrast and consistent letter spacing.

Prefer a consistent type scale and generous line-height. The combination of a warm palette and an editorial serif accent should feel contemporary, not rustic or vintage. Avoid tiny uppercase labels, excessive letter spacing, decorative script fonts, and long paragraphs with narrow line-height. Use a system font stack if external font loading adds unnecessary complexity.

### Layout and graphic language

- Desktop content width: approximately 1120–1200 px, with occasional full-bleed section backgrounds or media.
- Use a 12-column or equivalent grid for major sections.
- Allow selected project previews to overlap or offset within the grid, but keep the reading order clear.
- Use thin rules, numbered annotations, small captions, date labels, and restrained diagram-like details.
- Draw inspiration from field notebooks, topographic lines, geological layers, botanical studies, paper samples, and scientific annotations. Use these only as subtle visual motifs, not as decoration on every section.
- Use supplied presentations, posters, flyers, article covers, research images, and project documentation as key visual assets.
- Do not invent data charts, maps, scientific diagrams, or project imagery. Any chart or diagram must be based on source material or clearly labelled as a decorative abstract graphic with no factual claims.
- Do not use generic stock photos of leaves, wind turbines, solar panels, laboratories, or people as substitutes for her own work.
- Icons should be minimal and functional. Do not use emoji as interface icons.

### Image and texture direction

Prioritise her real work and personal project materials. Where additional imagery is genuinely needed, favour documentary-style images with natural light, restrained contrast, and authentic details such as landscapes, field notes, materials, research environments, or community settings. Avoid overly polished stock photography and images that imply she participated in a project when she did not.

If the site uses image treatments, keep them subtle: warm-neutral backgrounds, thin borders, soft corners, and consistent crops for gallery thumbnails. Do not apply heavy sepia filters to her actual documents or research images. Preserve the original colours of posters, charts, and presentation slides so the work remains accurate and legible.

## 5. Information architecture

### Main navigation

- Home
- Work
- About
- Contact

Include a visible CV link or button when an approved CV file is available. On small screens, use an accessible mobile menu.

### Page 1: Home

Purpose: establish her scientific background, environmental direction, and strongest evidence quickly.

Recommended sections:

1. **Hero**
   - Name: `[HER NAME]`
   - Short descriptor: a factual description of her physics and medical physics background, followed by her environmental project direction.
   - Headline direction: “Turning scientific thinking into practical environmental action.”
   - Supporting statement explaining that she is exploring or pursuing environmental and sustainability project roles.
   - Primary action: `Explore my work`.
   - Secondary action: `Get in touch` or `View CV`, depending on approved assets.
   - Optional portrait only if she wants one and supplies a suitable photo.

2. **Professional snapshot**
   - Physics education.
   - Approximately three years of medical physics experience, with exact role, organisation, and dates only when confirmed.
   - A concise view of the professional capabilities demonstrated in her experience.
   - Her environmental project direction.

3. **Selected work**
   - Feature three to five strong items across different formats.
   - Include a mix of project work and communication/research outputs where possible.
   - Prioritise relevance, quality, and clarity over the number of cards.
   - Show work type, title, short context, and year where known.

4. **How I work / What I bring**
   - Three or four evidence-backed strengths, such as research and analysis, project support, technical communication, documentation, coordination, or visual storytelling.
   - Every strength must be supported by a real CV entry or portfolio example.
   - Link strengths to the relevant work where possible.

5. **Research and communication strip**
   - Use a visual selection of presentation slides, posters, flyers, article covers, or research outputs.
   - This section should make her ability to turn complex material into clear communication immediately visible.

6. **About preview**
   - A short explanation of her academic and professional background and why she is moving towards environmental work.

7. **Contact invitation**
   - A clear, low-friction way to contact her about opportunities or collaboration.

### Page 2: Work

Purpose: make her work easy to browse and evaluate. Use “Work” as the main navigation label so the page can contain research, communication, and projects without suggesting that every item is a formal project.

Use a visual gallery with filters only where there are enough items to make filtering useful. Suggested categories:

- Projects
- Research
- Presentations
- Posters and flyers
- Articles and writing

If the actual portfolio contains only a small number of items in a category, omit that filter rather than showing an empty category. Keep an “All work” option.

Each card should show:

- A real cover image or preview, if available.
- Title.
- Work type.
- Year, if confirmed.
- A short factual description.
- Optional topic or capability tags.
- A clear indication of whether it was professional, academic, independent, volunteer, or collaborative work when that context matters.

### Portfolio item detail

Use a consistent detail page or detail view. Prefer a dedicated page for substantial projects, articles, and research outputs so each item can be shared directly.

Use only the sections that apply and are supported by the source material:

- Title and work type.
- One-sentence summary.
- Context or objective.
- Her role and specific contribution.
- People or teams involved, where permission allows.
- Process, methods, or approach.
- Deliverables or output.
- Result or impact, only where documented.
- Skills demonstrated, linked to the work rather than generic claims.
- Reflection or relevance to her environmental direction, where appropriate.
- Tools or collaborators, only when confirmed and permitted to disclose.
- Embedded preview or a link to the original file.

For project-oriented work, explain the practical sequence where information exists: objective, planning or preparation, coordination or contribution, communication, delivery, and outcome. Do not invent project-management responsibilities or milestones.

### Page 3: About

Purpose: explain the person behind the work and make the career transition understandable and credible.

Recommended content:

- A short professional introduction.
- Education and relevant professional experience.
- What she learned from working in medical physics and how those capabilities transfer to broader project environments.
- Why she is interested in environmental and sustainability work, using her own words once provided.
- The project roles and environmental themes she is pursuing.
- A concise set of relevant capabilities, each grounded in evidence.
- A CV link if the CV is approved for public sharing.

Do not invent motivations, personal values, career goals, certifications, dates, or achievements.

### Page 4: Contact

Purpose: make professional contact straightforward.

Include only contact details she has agreed to publish, such as a professional email, LinkedIn profile, or another professional channel. A simple email link is sufficient for the first release. Do not build a contact form that requires a backend unless explicitly requested.

## 6. Work presentation requirements

The portfolio will contain different media types. Give each one a useful presentation treatment.

### Presentations

- Show a cover or representative slide as the card thumbnail.
- Provide an accessible way to open or download the PDF when permission allows.
- An embedded PDF viewer may be used on desktop, but always provide an obvious open/download fallback.
- On mobile, prioritise a reliable file link over a cramped embedded viewer.

### Posters and flyers

- Use image previews with a sensible aspect ratio and `object-fit: contain` where preserving the full design matters.
- Allow a larger view in an accessible lightbox or detail page.
- Never crop essential poster text from the main preview without also providing a full view.

### Articles and writing

- Prefer readable HTML article pages for pieces she is allowed to republish.
- For externally published articles, link to the original publisher and identify the publication accurately.
- Preserve title, date, publication, and authorship only when confirmed.

### Projects and research

- Use concise case studies rather than a wall of screenshots.
- State her exact role and contribution. Distinguish individual work from team work.
- Explain the context, approach, key activities, outputs, and outcome in plain language.
- Where a project involved coordination or delivery, state the actual planning, communication, documentation, scheduling, or stakeholder tasks she performed. Do not infer these from the project title.
- Include outcomes or metrics only when they are documented and approved for publication.

## 7. Responsive behaviour

Design mobile-first and test at narrow, medium, and wide viewports.

- Mobile: single-column content, readable type, compact navigation, full-width primary actions, and touch-friendly controls.
- Tablet: use two-column card grids where appropriate.
- Desktop: use the editorial grid, occasional asymmetry, and varied media layouts without making the page feel crowded.
- Avoid horizontal scrolling, clipped text, tiny metadata, and controls that rely on hover.
- Media previews should resize without distortion. Preserve the original aspect ratio unless the design explicitly calls for a cropped thumbnail.

## 8. Interaction and motion

- Keep interactions purposeful: navigation, filtering, opening work, viewing documents, and contacting her.
- Use subtle transitions for hover, focus, and opening overlays.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Every clickable element must have a clear keyboard focus state.
- Do not add parallax, elaborate scroll animations, custom cursors, or decorative motion that distracts from the work.

## 9. Accessibility and quality

Target WCAG 2.2 AA practices where practical.

- Use semantic landmarks and a logical heading hierarchy.
- Ensure colour contrast for text and interactive elements.
- Provide meaningful alt text for informative images. Use empty alt text for decorative images.
- Ensure keyboard access for navigation, gallery filters, dialogs, and document links.
- Make focus visible and focus order logical.
- Label links clearly. Avoid generic labels such as “click here”.
- Use captions or text descriptions when a visual alone does not communicate the work.
- Do not rely on colour alone to communicate category or status.

## 10. Content integrity, privacy, and permissions

This site may include material from medical physics work, research, universities, employers, or collaborators. Treat privacy and professional confidentiality as non-negotiable.

- Never publish patient information, identifying images, clinical records, private institutional information, or confidential project details.
- Do not expose unpublished research, internal documents, or proprietary material without explicit permission.
- Before publishing work created for an employer, university, client, or team, confirm that she has permission to display it.
- Remove personal data from document metadata where appropriate.
- Do not invent project outcomes, employers, clients, dates, publications, awards, certifications, skills, or tools.
- Do not present a team deliverable as her sole work. State her contribution accurately.
- If permission is uncertain, keep the asset private and flag it for owner review. Do not publish a redacted or recreated version without approval.

## 11. Content model

Keep portfolio content separate from page layout so new work can be added without rewriting components.

Each portfolio item should support fields equivalent to:

- `slug`
- `title`
- `type` (`project`, `research`, `presentation`, `poster`, `flyer`, `article`)
- `summary`
- `context` (optional)
- `role` or `contribution` (optional, factual)
- `year` (optional)
- `workContext` (`professional`, `academic`, `independent`, `volunteer`, or `collaborative`, optional)
- `thumbnail` (optional)
- `coverImageAlt` (optional)
- `tags` (optional)
- `featured` (boolean)
- `visibility` (`public`, `private`, or `draft`)
- `content` or `sections` (optional)
- `fileUrl` (optional)
- `externalUrl` (optional)
- `publication` and `authors` (optional, for articles)

The exact schema may be adapted to the existing stack. Do not expose private or draft items in the public interface.

## 12. Initial content placeholders

Use placeholders only where the owner must provide information. Do not make them look like finished content.

- `[HER NAME]`
- `[APPROVED PROFESSIONAL DESCRIPTOR]`
- `[SHORT INTRODUCTION]`
- `[EDUCATION AND INSTITUTION]`
- `[MEDICAL PHYSICS ROLE, ORGANISATION, AND DATES]`
- `[EVIDENCE-BACKED TRANSFERABLE CAPABILITIES]`
- `[ENVIRONMENTAL THEMES SHE WANTS TO PURSUE]`
- `[PROFESSIONAL EMAIL]`
- `[LINKEDIN URL]`
- `[APPROVED CV FILE]`
- `[PORTFOLIO ITEM DETAILS]`

Do not invent placeholder project names or fake statistics to make the page look populated. During development, use clearly labelled local sample content only if needed for layout testing, and remove it before delivery.

## 13. Definition of done

The first release is complete when:

- The site clearly communicates her physics and medical physics background and her intended move towards environmental project work.
- Visitors can browse available projects, research, presentations, posters, flyers, and articles.
- The site demonstrates both her ability to work with technical material and to communicate it visually and clearly.
- Each published item has accurate metadata and a working destination or preview.
- The site works on mobile, tablet, and desktop.
- Navigation, filters, dialogs, and links work with keyboard and touch.
- No unapproved or confidential material is exposed.
- No unsupported claims, fake metrics, or invented credentials appear.
- The project builds successfully and has no obvious runtime errors.
- The owner can add another portfolio item by editing content data and adding its asset, without redesigning the page.

## 14. Decisions to confirm with the owner

These remain open until she confirms them:

1. Her name and preferred professional descriptor.
2. The specific environmental themes and types of organisations she wants to work with.
3. Whether she wants a portrait on the site.
4. Which portfolio items are approved for public display and whether each was professional, academic, independent, volunteer, or collaborative work.
5. Whether she wants a public CV download and which contact channels to publish.
6. Which project examples best demonstrate planning, coordination, communication, teamwork, or delivery.
